to wound

30/m2.3
To cut or hurt someone's body physically
verb US / wund /

Examples

To wound a soldier

Words can wound as deeply as physical blows.

The soldier was wounded in battle.
The knife wounded him deeply.
The athlete was wounded during the match.
Did you really wound your brother during the game?

Etymology

wound as a verb meaning to injure comes from an old English word wund meaning 'injury' or 'sore', so it still means to make a painful cut or damage someone's body.
