Etymology

The idiom caught between the hammer and the anvil comes from a blacksmith's shop, where a heavy hammer hits hot metal placed on the anvil, so anything between them would be crushed. That image explains why it means 'being forced to choose between two equally bad or unpleasant options' or feeling pressure from opposite sides.
