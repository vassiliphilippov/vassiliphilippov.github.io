immaterial to (someone)

0.00/m7.1
A fact or detail that does not matter to someone
adjective US / ˌɪməˈtɪriəl tə ˈsʌmwʌn /

Examples

Immaterial to the decision
The manager's explanation was immaterial to the outcome of the negotiation.
Whether the meeting is at 10 or 11 is immaterial to me since I can't attend either time.
Your personal preference is immaterial to this decision; we must follow company policy.

Etymology

immaterial to comes from the word material, which used to mean 'important facts', and the little bit im- adds the idea 'not'. Imagine a small detail that does not change the result; that detail is 'not important' or immaterial to someone.
