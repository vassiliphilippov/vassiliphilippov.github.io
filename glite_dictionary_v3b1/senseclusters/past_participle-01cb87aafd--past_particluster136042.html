Etymology

The term past participle comes from two parts: past, meaning 'finished', and participle, a special verb form often used like an adjective. Think of it as the verb form that shows a completed action; we use it with 'have', for example 'I have eaten', to make perfect tenses, or with 'be', for example 'was eaten', to make the passive, so the past participle tells us something is finished or was done to someone or something.
