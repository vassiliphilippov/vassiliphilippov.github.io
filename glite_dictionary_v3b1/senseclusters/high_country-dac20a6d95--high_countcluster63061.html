Etymology

The noun high country names land that is high up, like mountain areas above the lower hills. The phrase comes from the picture of high meaning 'tall' and country meaning 'open land', so it refers to the 'high' part of the landscape where the air is cooler, snow may stay longer and different plants grow, and people go there for views and hiking.
