follow in someone's footsteps

1/m3.4
To do the same job as someone who came before you, for example a parent
verb US / ˈfɑːloʊ ɪn ˈsʌmwʌnz ˈfʊtˌstɛps /

Etymology

follow in someone's footsteps creates a clear picture: you walk behind someone and put your feet into their footsteps, so to follow them is to go the same way. That's why it now means 'to do the same thing as someone else', often a parent or teacher.
