Etymology

The idiom have a time of it comes from the image of someone forced to go through a hard time, like being stuck in a storm or trying to free a car from deep snow. One popular story says people used 'time' to mean a bad spell, so to have a time of it came to mean 'to experience considerable difficulty'.
