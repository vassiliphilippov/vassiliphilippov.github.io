Etymology

Synecdochically comes from synecdoche, which has the part syn- 'together' and a Greek element that means 'take', and it pictures a 'part' standing for the 'whole' or the whole for a part. So, to speak synecdochically is to use a part to mean the whole (like saying 'wheels' for a car) or to use the whole to mean a part (like 'the White House' for the U.S. administration).
