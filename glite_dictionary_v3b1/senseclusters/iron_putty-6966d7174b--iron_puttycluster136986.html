Etymology

iron putty comes from how the material looked and was used: craftsmen mixed iron powder like rust with boiled linseed oil to make a soft, paste-like putty that could be pressed into cracks. That's why iron putty now means a tough, 'acid-resistant' sealing paste used to fill and protect metal parts.
