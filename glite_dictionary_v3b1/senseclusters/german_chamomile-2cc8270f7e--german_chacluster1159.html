Etymology

German chamomile gets its name from the word chamomile, which one popular story says comes from old words meaning 'earth apple' because the low plant smells a little like apples and grows close to the ground; the 'German' part simply points to the plant being known and used in Germany and nearby areas. That's why German chamomile is remembered as a small, apple-scented herb used in calming teas and simple medicine today.
