Etymology

The word vicariate comes from the old Latin word vicarius, which means 'substitute' or 'in place of' and is the same root as in vicar and vicarious. A vicariate is the area or office that a vicar looks after as a substitute for a bishop, so the origin explains why it names the district under a vicar's care.
