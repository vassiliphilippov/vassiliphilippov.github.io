Etymology

fray at the edges comes from the image of a piece of cloth that starts to fray at its edges when it is worn and the threads come loose. Those loose threads make the cloth weaker, so the phrase is used when a situation or group slowly becomes 'weaker' or 'less effective' under pressure.
