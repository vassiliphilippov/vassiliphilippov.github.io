Etymology

Otaheite arrowroot gets its name from Otaheite (an old name for 'Tahiti') and arrowroot, the starchy root of the local 'pia' plant used in food. One popular story says people once used arrowroot on arrow 'wounds' to help them heal, so the image of a soft, healing powder helps explain why the name now means the cooking starch used to 'thicken' foods.
