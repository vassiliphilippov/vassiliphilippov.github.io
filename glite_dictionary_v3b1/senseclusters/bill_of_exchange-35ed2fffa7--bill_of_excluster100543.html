Etymology

The noun bill of exchange may come from old trade where a paper called a bill told one person or bank to 'pay' another. Traders would 'exchange' that paper instead of sending cash far away, so the paper acted as an order and a promise to pay, which is why today the phrase means a written order to pay a set sum at a set time.
