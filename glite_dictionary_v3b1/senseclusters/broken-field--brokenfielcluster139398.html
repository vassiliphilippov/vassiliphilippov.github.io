Etymology

broken-field comes from the two simple parts broken ('split' or 'not straight') and field ('open area'); it gives a clear picture of a play that is interrupted by opponents. So, broken-field describes a style of running or movement where you must make sudden turns and changes of direction to avoid obstacles or defenders.
