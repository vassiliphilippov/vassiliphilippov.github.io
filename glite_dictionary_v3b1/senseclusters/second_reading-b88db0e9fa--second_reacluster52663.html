Etymology

The noun second reading comes from the old practice of reading a proposed law aloud a second time in meetings where people make laws. The words second and reading point to this second time, and because the bill was read again to explain its 'main ideas' or hear a committee report, second reading now means the second formal chance to 'discuss' or 'approve' a law.
