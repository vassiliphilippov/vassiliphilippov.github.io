Etymology

The noun crustal plate comes from a simple picture: the Earth's outer crust is like a hard shell that breaks into large, flat plates, like big dinner plates floating on softer rock below. That's why crustal plate means a large, hard part of the Earth's outer layer that slowly 'moves' and causes earthquakes where plates meet.
