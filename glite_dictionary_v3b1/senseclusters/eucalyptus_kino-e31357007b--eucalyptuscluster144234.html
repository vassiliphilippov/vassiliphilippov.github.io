Etymology

Eucalyptus kino is named for the eucalyptus tree and the old word kino for a dark gum; when the tree's bark is broken it oozes a red-brown sap that dries into a hard gum. People collected this gum to 'treat leather' and to make simple medicines, so today the term means the dried reddish-brown resin used in tanning and 'traditional medicine'.
