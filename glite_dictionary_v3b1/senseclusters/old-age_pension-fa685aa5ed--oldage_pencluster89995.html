Etymology

old-age pension may come from a long-time practice where rulers or employers gave retired soldiers or servants a small regular payment so they could live after they stopped work. The parts old-age (being old) and pension (a steady pay) create the image, so the phrase now means 'a regular payment made by the government or an employer to a person who has retired because of age'.
