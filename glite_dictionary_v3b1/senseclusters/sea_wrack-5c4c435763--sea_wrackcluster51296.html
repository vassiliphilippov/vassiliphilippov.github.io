Etymology

sea wrack comes from the simple image of seaweed the sea leaves on the beach: sea plus wrack, where 'wrack' is a word for things the water has washed up. So sea wrack means 'large seaweed or marine plants washed up on the shore', which is why you see it in heaps after a storm.
