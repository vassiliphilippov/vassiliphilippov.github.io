Etymology

inoculative comes from the Latin inoculare, which means 'to graft in', and it is the same root as English inoculate. A helpful image is putting a small plant bud into a tree to join it and give the tree a new part. So a vaccine is like a tiny, safe 'graft' of a germ that teaches the body to fight illness, and inoculative means able to give or produce immunity.
