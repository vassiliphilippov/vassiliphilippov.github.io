Etymology

gobbledygook was invented in the 1940s by a US government writer and comes from the English verb gobble (the noisy 'gobble' a turkey makes) plus a silly ending, so it sounds like messy, hard-to-understand talk. That's why gobbledygook means language full of big words and jargon that hides the real idea.
