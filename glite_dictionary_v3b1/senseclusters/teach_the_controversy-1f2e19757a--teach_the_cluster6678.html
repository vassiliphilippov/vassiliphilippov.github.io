Etymology

The phrase teach the controversy comes from debates about what to include in school lessons. Imagine a teacher writing both sides of a disputed topic on the board—literally teach the controversy—so students hear opposing views. That's why it now means 'to present or encourage discussion of opposing viewpoints'.
