Etymology

Japanese cedar may come from early foreign visitors who saw Japan's tall evergreen and called it a cedar because it looked and smelled like the cedars they knew. Because people long used its soft, light wood for temples and furniture, the name now refers to the tree from Japan that is valued for its wood.
