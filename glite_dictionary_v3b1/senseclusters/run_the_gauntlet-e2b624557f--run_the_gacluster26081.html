Etymology

The idiom run the gauntlet may come from an old military punishment where a person had to run between two rows of soldiers called a gauntlet, and the soldiers would hit them as they passed. So, the strong image of being attacked again and again is why the phrase now means 'to face a series of hard or unpleasant attacks or criticism'.
