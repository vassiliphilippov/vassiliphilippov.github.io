Etymology

complementary DNA comes from the idea that the new DNA is made to 'complement' or match a strand of messenger RNA. Scientists copy the RNA into DNA so it fits like a mirror or a puzzle piece, and that's why complementary DNA means DNA made to match RNA and is used to study which genes are active.
