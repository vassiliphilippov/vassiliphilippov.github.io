Etymology

no better than one ought to be comes from old moral talk where people compared someone to how one ought to be, the usual standard of proper behavior. Saying someone is no better than that creates the image that they are only as good as they should be, often meaning they acted badly, so the phrase means 'not as well-behaved or moral as expected'.
