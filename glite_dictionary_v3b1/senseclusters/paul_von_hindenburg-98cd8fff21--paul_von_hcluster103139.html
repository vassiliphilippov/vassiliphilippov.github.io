Etymology

Paul von Hindenburg was a German army leader and later the country's president in the early 1900s. In 1933 he 'appointed' Adolf Hitler as 'chancellor', a decision that helped Hitler gain power, so Hindenburg's name is often linked to that key turning point in German history.
