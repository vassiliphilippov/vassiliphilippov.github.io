Etymology

Xerographic copier comes from words that mean xero 'dry' and graphic 'writing', so the name literally suggests 'dry writing'. Early machines used light to make a picture on an electrically charged drum, and dry powder stuck to that picture and was moved to the paper, so the name fits and today it means 'a machine that makes copies using a dry, light-based process'.
