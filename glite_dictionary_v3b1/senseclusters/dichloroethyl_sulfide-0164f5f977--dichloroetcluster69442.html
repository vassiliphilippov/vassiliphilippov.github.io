Etymology

dichloroethyl sulfide is named from its parts: di- means 'two', chloro- means 'chlorine', ethyl is 'a small carbon part', and sulfide means 'sulfur'. This chemical is better known as 'mustard gas' and was used in war because it causes severe blisters and breathing damage, so the name points to a dangerous substance.
