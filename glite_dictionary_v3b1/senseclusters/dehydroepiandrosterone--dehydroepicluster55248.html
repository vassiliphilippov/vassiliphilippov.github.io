Etymology

dehydroepiandrosterone comes from parts that describe its chemical nature: de- 'remove', hydro- 'water', epi- 'on', andro- 'man' (as in android), and sterone 'a type of hormone'. So the long name points to a hormone linked to 'andro' (male) chemistry that the body uses to help make both male and female sex hormones.
