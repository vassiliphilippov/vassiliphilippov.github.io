Etymology

The word gentleman-at-arms comes from two parts: gentleman, which once meant 'a noble or well-born man', and at-arms, which means 'ready with weapons'. So a gentleman-at-arms was originally a noble who served as the ruler's soldier, and today the title names a special ceremonial guard who stands by the king or queen.
