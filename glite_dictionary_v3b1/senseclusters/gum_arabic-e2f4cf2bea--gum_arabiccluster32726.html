Etymology

Gum Arabic comes from the sticky sap of acacia trees that was gathered and sold by traders from Arabia and nearby lands. The name shows the parts gum, meaning sticky tree sap, and Arabic, meaning from those traders, so today gum arabic refers to a natural, sticky substance used as a 'thickener' or 'binder' in food, sweets, and medicines.
