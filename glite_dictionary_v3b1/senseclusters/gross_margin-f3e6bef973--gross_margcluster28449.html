Etymology

The term gross margin comes from two simple ideas: gross meaning the total before costs and margin meaning the small amount left at the edge. Imagine sales as a cake and the cost to make a product as the piece taken away; the margin is the slice you keep. That's why it means the part of each sale that becomes 'profit' before other costs, usually shown as a percent.
