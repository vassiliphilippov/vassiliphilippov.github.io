Etymology

traveler's tree got its name from a simple, useful trick: the plant's large, fan-shaped leaves have thick stalks that hold clear sap people could 'drink' in an emergency. One popular story says travelers used that sap as a 'water source' on long journeys, so the plant became known as the traveler's tree.

Description: a tall tropical, palm-like plant (Ravenala madagascariensis) native to Madagascar, with fan-shaped leaves, known for its edible nuts and for leafstalks that store clear sap which can provide drinkable water in emergencies; reputedly used by travelers as an emergency water source
