Etymology

The term isogonic line comes from parts meaning iso for 'equal' and gonic for 'angle', and it describes an imaginary line on a map that joins places where a compass points the same amount away from 'true north'. So, following an isogonic line keeps the 'compass error' the same, which helps pilots and sailors correct their direction.
