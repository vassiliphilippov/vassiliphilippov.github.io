Etymology

kettle hole may come from the Ice Age. Big blocks of ice sat in soft ground and later melted, leaving round pits that often filled with water. Those pits looked like a deep kettle, a 'cooking pot', so people called them kettle holes, and today the name means a water-filled 'hollow' in the ground.
