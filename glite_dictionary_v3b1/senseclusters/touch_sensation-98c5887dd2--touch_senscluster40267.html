Etymology

touch sensation comes from the simple idea of two parts: touch (when something touches your skin) and sensation (a feeling or signal you notice). It pictures tiny nerves in the skin sending a signal to your brain when you are touched, so the phrase now means 'the physical feeling when something comes into contact with the skin'.
