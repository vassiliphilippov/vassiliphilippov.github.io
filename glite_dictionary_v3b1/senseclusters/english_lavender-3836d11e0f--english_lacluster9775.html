Etymology

English lavender comes from a type of lavender that gardeners in England grew for its sweet, clean smell, and people long ago put its flowers in baths and dried them to scent clothes. That's why English lavender today means a small, fragrant shrub with lilac flowers people dry or use for 'fragrance' and 'sachets'.
