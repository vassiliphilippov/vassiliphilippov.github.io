Etymology

East Indian rosewood gets its name from where the wood came from and how it smelled: traders called dark wood from the 'East Indies', an old name for parts of South Asia, rosewood because it sometimes smelled like roses when it was cut. So the name joins East/Indian (where the tree grew) with rosewood (wood that smells like roses), and that is why it means 'a valuable Indian wood used for fine furniture and musical instruments'.
