Etymology

threadbare comes from thread ('string in cloth') and bare ('no covering'), which paints a picture of fabric worn thin until it is exposed. A helpful way to remember the other meaning is to think of an idea or phrase 'worn out' by too much use, so threadbare also means unoriginal or stale.
