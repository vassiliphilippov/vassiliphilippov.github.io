Etymology

The noun gaff-headed sail comes from the pole called a gaff that holds up the sail's head, or 'top edge', so the sail is four-sided instead of a simple triangle. That's why a gaff-headed sail means a sail with a gaff at the top — the gaff gives a straight top and extra support, making the sail easier to shape and handle on a boat.
