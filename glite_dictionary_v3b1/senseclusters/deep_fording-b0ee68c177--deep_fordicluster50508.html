Etymology

The noun deep fording comes from early travelers who had to wade through parts of a river where the water was much deeper than usual; deep shows the water level and fording means crossing the river on foot or by horse. So, deep fording means 'the act or place of crossing a river or stream where the water is deep', and it was seen as a risky crossing.
