Etymology

Basso continuo comes from the Italian words basso ('low sound') and continuo ('continuous'), and it names a steady low line used in older classical music. Players such as a cello and a keyboard would play that low line the whole time, with numbers written to show the chords, so it keeps the harmony. That's why basso continuo means 'a continuous bass part that supports the melody and harmony'.
