Etymology

The phrase poor man's shilling may come from times when a shilling was a small coin and a poor person could only afford a little money, so the phrase paints the image of a cheap, small thing used instead of something better. That's why people use poor man's shilling today to mean 'a low-cost, lower-quality investment'.
