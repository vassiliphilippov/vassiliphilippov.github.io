Etymology

The phrase sear the meat comes from a simple kitchen picture: when you searmeat you put it into a very hot pan so the outside quickly turns brown and makes a crust. That's why it now means 'to quickly cook the outside of meat at high heat to make a browned crust' and often helps keep the juices inside.
