Etymology

The phrasal verb traipse through comes from an old word: traipse once meant 'to drag' or 'to tramp' and through means across a place. So imagine someone slowly wandering with their feet dragging across streets or rooms, and that picture explains why traipse through means 'to walk or wander casually'.
