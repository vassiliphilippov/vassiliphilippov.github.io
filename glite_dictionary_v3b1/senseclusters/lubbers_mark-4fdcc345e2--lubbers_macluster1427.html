Etymology

The term lubber's mark comes from old sailors and names the fixed line on a ship's compass that shows the ship's direction. Because lubber once meant a clumsy or new sailor, the simple mark was an easy guide for anyone, so the phrase now means the fixed line that shows the ship's 'heading'.
