Etymology

glottal catch comes from the idea of the voice briefly 'catching' in the throat: speakers close the glottal area, the vocal cords, and then open them to make a sudden short sound, so the word catch shows that quick stop. That's why glottal catch names the sharp throat sound some accents use before a vowel or instead of the 't' sound.
