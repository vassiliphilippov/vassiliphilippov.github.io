Etymology

The word junta comes from Spanish where it meant 'a meeting' or 'a joined group', and from the old Latin root jungere meaning 'to join', the same idea we see in English words like join and joint. So a junta is a 'joined group', which is why the word now often means a small group of military officers who take control of a government.
