Etymology

The phrase purple passage may come from the old idea that purple cloth was rare and worn by kings and queens, so it looked rich and showy. A passage is a short part of writing, so a purple passage is a part that uses very rich, 'flowery' language, and that's why it now means a section that is too 'showy' or 'overdone'.
