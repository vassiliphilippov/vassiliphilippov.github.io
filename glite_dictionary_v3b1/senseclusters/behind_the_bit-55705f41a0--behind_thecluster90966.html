Etymology

The phrase behind the bit comes from riding: it describes a horse that pulls its head back and avoids the bit, the metal piece in its mouth, so the rider loses clear contact. That's why saying a horse is behind the bit means it is 'not responsive' or 'restricted' and not moving well for the rider.
