Etymology

closed-end fund The noun closed-end fund comes from the idea that the fund is closed after it sells shares and has a fixed end, meaning a set number of shares. Because it does not make new shares, the existing shares are 'traded on the stock market', so the term means an investment with a 'fixed number of shares' that people buy and sell like stocks.
