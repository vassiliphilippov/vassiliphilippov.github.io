Etymology

The noun direct tide comes from the simple image of the moon pulling the sea, so the water rises on the side facing the moon, the direct side, and this rise is a high tide. A matching rise on the opposite side makes a second 'high tide', so the name helps explain why many coasts have two high tides each day.
