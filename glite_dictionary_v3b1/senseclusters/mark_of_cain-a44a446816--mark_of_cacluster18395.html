Etymology

mark of Cain comes from a Bible story: after Cain killed his brother Abel, God put a mark on Cain to show he was 'guilty' and to make him an 'outcast'. So today the phrase means someone who is seen as 'guilty', 'shamed', or an 'outcast'.
