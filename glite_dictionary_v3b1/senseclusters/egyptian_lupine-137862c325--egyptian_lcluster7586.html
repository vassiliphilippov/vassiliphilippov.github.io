Etymology

Egyptian lupine joins Egyptian (where it was long grown) and lupine, a word from an old term for 'wolf'. People once thought the plant 'ate' poor soil like a wolf, but it actually holds soil and adds nutrients, so the name helps remember this white herb used to stop erosion and feed animals.
