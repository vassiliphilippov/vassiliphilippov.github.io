Etymology

at someone's feet comes from the old image of a person sitting or kneeling by someone's feet to show respect or to learn from them. Being literally at someone's feet meant you were close, ready to obey and admiring, so the phrase now means 'in a position of loyalty, admiration, or submission to someone'.
