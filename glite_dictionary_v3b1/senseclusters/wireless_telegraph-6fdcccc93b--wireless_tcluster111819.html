Etymology

The noun wireless telegraph comes from the idea of a telegraph that is wireless, sending messages through the air instead of along wires. In the early days people sent Morse code by radio so ships and stations could 'talk' over long distances, so today the phrase means 'sending telegraph messages by radio'.
