Etymology

ring-bark joins ring ('a complete circle') and bark ('the tree's outer skin'), and it means to cut a circle of bark all the way around a trunk. Doing this removes the living layer that moves food in the tree, so the tree usually dies — that's why to ring-bark a tree is to kill it.
