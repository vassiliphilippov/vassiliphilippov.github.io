Etymology

The idiom pay one's pound of flesh comes from Shakespeare's play 'The Merchant of Venice', where a man who lent money insisted on taking a real pound of his debtor's flesh as payment. That shocking image explains why the phrase now means 'to be forced to meet a very harsh or unfair demand'.
