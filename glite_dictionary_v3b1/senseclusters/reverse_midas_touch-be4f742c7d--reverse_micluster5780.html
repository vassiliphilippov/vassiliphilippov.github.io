Etymology

The noun reverse Midas touch plays on the old story of King Midas, who turned everything he touched into gold with the original Midas touch. Adding 'reverse' imagines the opposite: everything someone touches goes wrong, so the phrase means 'a tendency to cause failure or ruin'.
