Contrary statements
In logic, two contrary statements cannot both be true, although both may be false.
The terms 'hot' and 'cold' are often cited as classic contrary pairs in semantics.
Unlike contradictory propositions, both contrary propositions can be simultaneously false without contradiction.

Etymology

contrary comes from the Latin root contra meaning 'against', keeping the sense of 'opposite'. So when two statements are in this relation, they are called contrary because they stand 'against' one another and cannot both be true.
