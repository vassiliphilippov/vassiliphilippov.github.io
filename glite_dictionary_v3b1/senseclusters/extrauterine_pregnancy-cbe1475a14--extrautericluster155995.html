Etymology

The medical term extrauterine pregnancy comes from extra, meaning 'outside', and uterine, relating to the 'uterus' or 'womb'. So it describes a pregnancy that grows 'outside' the 'uterus', often in a tube next to the womb, and means a pregnancy that can be dangerous and needs quick treatment.
