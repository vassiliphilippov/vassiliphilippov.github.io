Etymology

Marcus Tullius Cicero gets his name from the family surname Cicero, which in Latin means a 'chickpea' and, one story says, came from an ancestor with a wart that looked like one. He became famous for his speeches and writing, so the odd name helps us remember the great Roman 'speaker' and 'statesman'.
