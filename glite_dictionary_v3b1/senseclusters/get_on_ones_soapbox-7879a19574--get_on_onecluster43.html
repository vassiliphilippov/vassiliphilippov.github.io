Etymology

The phrase get on one's soapbox comes from a simple picture: people used to stand on a wooden soapbox, a box that had held soap, so they could be higher and speak to a crowd. So today, to get on one's soapbox means 'to speak publicly or for a long time' about your opinions, often in a strong or excited way.
