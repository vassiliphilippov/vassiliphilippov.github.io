Etymology

The term drag coefficient comes from two parts: drag, the push of air or water that tries to slow something, and coefficient, a simple number that measures how strong that push is. So it is like a score that tells 'how easily something moves through air or water', with a low number meaning less push and easier movement.

Description: A dimensionless number representing the ratio of the drag force acting on an object moving through a fluid to the product of the fluid density, the object's reference area, and the square of the velocity. It measures how easily an object moves through air or another fluid.
