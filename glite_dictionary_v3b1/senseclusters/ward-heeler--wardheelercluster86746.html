Etymology

ward-heeler joins ward ('a local voting area') and heeler (from heel, which can mean 'to follow' or a 'troublemaker'). A helpful way to remember it is to picture someone who 'heels' through a ward doing small favors and pressure to win votes, so a ward-heeler today is a local political worker who delivers votes for a party.
