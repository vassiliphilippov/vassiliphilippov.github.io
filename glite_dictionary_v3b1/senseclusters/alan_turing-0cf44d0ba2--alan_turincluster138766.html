Etymology

Alan Turing belongs to a real English mathematician and codebreaker from the 1900s. He created the idea of the Turing machine, which is like a very simple machine that reads and writes marks on a long strip of paper to follow rules and act like a tiny 'brain'. He also helped break German codes during World War II, and that's why his name now stands for 'computer science' and the idea of machines that can 'think'.
