Etymology

sally out comes from soldiers in old forts who would suddenly rush out to attack. The picture of people rushing out from behind the walls, a quick bold move like a leap that is the idea of sally, explains why the phrase means 'to leave a place suddenly and energetically', often to 'attack', 'surprise', or go on a short trip.
