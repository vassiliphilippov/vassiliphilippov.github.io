Etymology

seamer comes from seam ('line where two pieces meet') and the agent -er ('one who'), so a seamer is someone or a machine that joins pieces by making a seam. That explains why factories call their sewing or sealing machines 'seamers' and why a skilled person who joins fabrics is also a seamer.
