Etymology

Centaurea gymnocarpa comes from a mix of myth and a simple plant description. The first part, Centaurea, recalls the centaur from old stories who knew and used plants, and the second part, gymnocarpa, literally means 'naked fruit' — imagine a small, bare seed head. That's why the name points to this silvery, downy plant found on some Mediterranean islands.
