Etymology

Asarum canadense gets its scientific name from the old name Asarum, used for wild ginger, and the word canadense, which means 'from Canada'. Because its root smells like ginger, people call it 'wild ginger', so the name helps you remember it's a ginger-like groundcover from Canada and the eastern United States.
